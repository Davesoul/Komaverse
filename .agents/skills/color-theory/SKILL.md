# Color Theory for Cel Work

Value does the work, hue gets the credit. If the greyscale fails, no
palette saves it — check value first, always.

## Think in three axes

- Hue: which color family. Keep the count low — two or three families
  per frame plus neutrals.
- Value: light vs dark. This carries form and focal hierarchy.
  Cel shading lives here: base, one shadow step, rare highlight.
- Chroma: saturation punch. Spend it like money: one saturated accent
  against muted surroundings, or the frame screams everywhere.

## Cel shadow colors (the anime rule)

- Shadows are never black or grey fill. Shadow = base hue shifted
  toward its cool complement and darkened: skin shadows go mauve or
  cool brown, blue cloth shadows go deep indigo, warm scenes shadow
  toward purple.
- Reflected light: bounce a whisper of the environment into shadows
  (green under trees, warm under sunset). A touch only.
- Highlights: warm white or the light-source color, placed on upward
  planes, hair blocks, and eyes. Fewer than you think.

## Harmony schemes that work

- Complementary (orange/teal, red/cyan): maximum pop for action and
  key visuals. Mute one side or it vibrates.
- Analogous (red/orange/yellow): warm unity for cozy, sunset, memory
  scenes.
- Triadic with a dominant: pick three anchors, let one family own
  70 percent of the frame.
- Limited palette: 4 to 6 swatches total for retro-OVA and manga-color
  work. Constraint reads as style.

## Light logic

- One key light per scene with a named source and direction. Every
  shadow shape in the frame must agree with it.
- Warm light / cool shadow (golden hour) or cool light / warm shadow
  (night interior) — never white light with grey shadow.
- Night is dark blue, not black: keep values readable, push
  silhouettes against a lighter sky band.
- Sunset, rain, and snow each get one signature treatment per project
  and keep it (see entities as style anchors).

## Mood control

- High-key (bright, low contrast): safety, comedy, morning.
- Low-key (dark, one light source): tension, mystery, night ops.
- Desaturate for memory, grief, and flashback; oversaturate one
  accent for obsession, power-ups, and alarm.
- Skin stays believable: never green or grey without a stated
  in-world light source doing it.

## Writing color into prompts

- Name exact colors (hair #2b2b3a, eyes #d64545), not vibes.
- State the scheme: "triadic palette, indigo dominant, single warm
  accent" beats "colorful".
- Lock light: "single warm key light upper left, cool violet shadows".
- Forbid the failure mode: "no airbrush gradients, no muddy midtones,
  hard cel shadow edges".

## QC before accepting

- Greyscale read: three clear value groups.
- Shadow hues shifted, never black fill.
- One light source, every shadow agrees.
- Skin believable, accent count under three.
- Exact hexes match the entity anchors.
