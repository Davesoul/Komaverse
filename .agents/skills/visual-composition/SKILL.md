# Visual Composition for Frames and Panels

Composition is the order you show things in. A viewer reads a frame in
under a second: biggest contrast first, then faces, then motion. Every
rule below serves that single pass.

## The one-pass read

1. One focal point per frame. Two heroes fight; pick the winner with
   size, contrast, or isolation.
2. Biggest contrast (light vs dark, saturated vs grey) marks the focal
   point. Check by squinting: the focal shape must survive the blur.
3. Faces pull the eye before anything else. Place them deliberately or
   they hijack the read.

## Structure

- Thirds and grids: put the focal point on a third intersection, not
  dead center, unless the beat is confrontation (centered = impact).
- Leading lines: roads, arms, blades, speed lines, panel borders aimed
  at the focal point. Diagonal lines add energy; horizontals calm.
- Framing devices: foreground silhouettes (branches, doorframes, crowd
  heads) that box the subject and add depth for free.
- Depth in three planes: a dark foreground shape, a midground subject,
  a lighter background. Flat anime backgrounds still need the planes.
- Headroom and look room: leave space in front of a gaze or a motion
  vector, never behind it.

## Contrast and weight

- Value first, hue second. A red-on-green clash still fails if both
  share one grey. Squint-test every key frame.
- Saturated color is heavy: small accents balance large neutrals.
- Detail is heavy too: rest the eye with one quiet area per frame.
  Busy everywhere means focus nowhere.

## Staging for anime cuts

- One cut, one idea. A cut that changes subject AND camera AND lighting
  needs splitting.
- Enter moving: subjects cross frame borders on action, not from rest.
- Reaction inserts are close and centered: break the thirds rule on
  purpose so the beat pops against the staged masters.
- Match eyelines and screen direction across the cut or the geography
  collapses.

## Manga panel composition

- Panel size is emphasis: splash for arrival, tall thin for falling or
  speed, stacked small for rapid exchange.
- Bleed (art off the panel edge) for impact; bordered calm for order.
- Gutter rhythm is timing: wide gutters pause, narrow gutters rush.
- Keep the action axis consistent inside a scene; flip it only to
  disorient on purpose.

## QC before accepting

- Squint test: focal point survives.
- One idea per frame/cut/panel.
- Look room in front of every gaze and motion.
- No tangent lines touching exactly (reposition or overlap clearly).
- Value sketch reads in three greys before any color goes on.
