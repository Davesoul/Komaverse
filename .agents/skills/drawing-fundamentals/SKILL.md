# Drawing Fundamentals for the Studio

Draw construction first, detail last. Every correction below is cheaper
at the construction stage than after rendering.

## Construction

- Build everything from simple solids: head as sphere + jaw wedge,
  torso as bean, limbs as tapered cylinders, hands as mitten + finger
  wedges.
- Gesture before anatomy: one or two flowing lines for the action,
  then hang the solids on them. Noodle-stiff poses come from skipping
  this.
- Symmetry check: flip the canvas (or the mental image) — errors hide
  in one orientation only.

## Line quality

- Confident single strokes beat scratchy multi-strokes. Draw through
  the form (complete the ellipse) then keep the good segment.
- Vary weight with intent: thicker outside silhouettes, thinner inside
  detail, tapered ends on speed strokes.
- In this studio the pen presets map to this directly: G-pen for
  silhouette and hair masses, mapping pen for faces and hands, brush
  pen for cloth and effects. Match the preset to the pass.
- Pencil owns construction and soft values: light HB or blue-lead
  layout lines under the ink (kept on the construction layer as the
  QC record, never erased first), and pencil hatching for gentle
  gradients where a hard cel shadow would be too harsh.

## Perspective that holds

- One-point for corridors, roads, confrontations down an axis.
  Two-point for most exteriors and rooms. Three-point looking up
  (power) or down (vertigo) only.
- Set ONE horizon line per scene and keep every box on it. Floating
  boxes are the classic giveaway.
- Foreshortening: overlap shapes and compress spacing toward the
  viewer. A fist near the camera is bigger than the head behind it —
  draw the size difference, do not average it.
- Ellipses follow the horizon: shallow far, round near. Wheels,
  cups, and eyes in profile obey this.

## Anime proportions

- Standard hero: 6 to 6.5 heads tall. Shojo leans 7, chibi 2 to 3.
  Pick one ratio per character and keep it (see entities as model
  sheets).
- Eyes sit on the horizontal midline of the head, not above it.
- Hands are three-quarter head length. Too-small hands are the most
  common off-model flag; count fingers before finishing any frame.

## Shading a drawing

- Cel rule: one shadow shape with a hard edge, decided by one light
  direction per scene. Second tone only for drama.
- Screentone for manga greys: pick one density per value step, keep
  the angle fixed per page, never mix dot and line tone on one form.
- Highlights last and few: hair blocks, eyes, wet surfaces only.

## Layer discipline (maps to manage_layers)

- One job per layer: sketch, lineart, flat color, shadow, tone, FX.
  Never ink on the flat-color layer.
- Name layers by content (hair-front, jacket-shadow), lock finished
  ones, merge only at export.
- Keep a construction layer at low opacity under the lineart instead
  of erasing the sketch — it is the QC record.
- Blend modes with intent: multiply for shadows and tones, screen or
  add for glows and impact flashes, normal for everything else.

## QC before accepting

- Flip test, horizon test (all boxes agree), hand test (count).
- One light direction, hard shadow edges.
- Line weight varies silhouette > interior.
- Layers named, locked where finished, no ink on color layers.
