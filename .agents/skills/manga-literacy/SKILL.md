# Reading Manga and Comics

A page is a time machine: panel size, order, and gutters control reading
speed. Adapt or generate pages to control time, not just to fill space.

## Reading direction

- Japanese manga reads right to left, top to bottom, including panel
  order inside the page. Western comics mirror this left to right.
  Never mix the two on one page.
- Action and eyelines must obey the direction: a chase that runs
  against the reading flow fights the reader on every panel.
- The studio default is RTL with an explicit toggle; confirm the
  direction before detecting, ordering, or lettering.

## Panel transitions (what changes between panels)

- Moment to moment: slow motion, footsteps, drips. Use sparingly.
- Action to action: one subject moving — the workhorse of fights
  and chases.
- Subject to subject: same scene, new focus — conversations and
  reactions.
- Scene to scene: new place or time — needs an establishing beat.
- Aspect to aspect: mood details with no time passing — rain, a
  clock, a face in the crowd. Pure atmosphere.
- Non-sequitur: deliberate shock only.

## Page architecture

- The page turn is the biggest beat: land reveals, gags, and scares
  on the new recto, never stranded before the turn.
- Splash pages buy scale at the cost of pace — one per scene at most.
- Tier rhythm: even tiers cruise, broken tiers (a tall panel
  interrupting the grid) punch.
- Bleed panels break the frame for impact; use one language per
  scene (all bleeds or none).

## Balloons and lettering

- Read order inside a panel follows the page direction: first balloon
  top-right in RTL, top-left in LTR.
- Tails must touch the speaker or clearly point; crossing tails tangle
  the dialogue.
- Shout shapes (jagged, oversized) are loud; thought clouds are quiet;
  narration boxes sit outside the action, top of panel.
- Never cover faces or hands with balloons. Plan empty space for
  lettering during composition, not after.

## Kakimoji SFX

- Sound effects are drawing, not text: oversized, distorted,
  integrated with the art (cracks run through the effect).
- Core reads: DON (impact), GASHAN (shatter), ZA (slash hiss),
  DOKI (heartbeat), SHIIN (silence — draw it big and empty).
- SFX obey perspective like everything else; flat floating SFX
  breaks the scene.

## Screentone grammar

- Tone = value + texture: dots for smooth grey, lines for speed and
  shadow direction, solids for weight.
- One angle per value family per page; moire comes from mixing.
- Flashbacks, phone calls, and inner monologue each get a signature
  tone treatment, kept consistent per project.

## Adapting pages to timeline

- One panel is one shot only when the panel holds one beat; split
  multi-beat panels into two timeline clips.
- Gutter width becomes hold time: wide gutters linger, narrow gutters
  cut fast.
- Aspect-to-aspect panels become 0.5 to 1 second inserts over the
  scene, not full clips.
- SFX panels land on the audio hit; silence panels (SHIIN) play
  under room tone or nothing at all.

## QC before accepting

- Read the page aloud in the right direction without stumbling.
- Every tail touches its speaker; no balloon covers a face.
- SFX in perspective, tones consistent per page.
- Page-turn pays off; splash count justified.
