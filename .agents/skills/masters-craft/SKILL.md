# Masters craft: steal the technique, never the character

Study principles, never reproduce protected characters, exact
designs, or franchise plots. "In the spirit of X's Y" means the
working method below — original characters only, always.

Pick ONE lens per job. Two lenses is a hybrid with no author.

## Manga masters

- **Osamu Tezuka (cinematic manga)** — invented film grammar on
  paper: close-up / insert / pan treated as cuts, star-system cast
  reused across stories. Apply: vary shot scale every 2–3 panels;
  inserts of hands/objects instead of another talking head.
- **Katsuhiro Otomo (widescreen realism)** — expansive horizontal
  panels, meticulously hand-hatched ruins and machinery, photo and
  cinema composition translated to ink, speed controlled by density
  (rush the chase, freeze the impact). Apply: wide establishing
  panels, fine-line backgrounds that stay behind the action.
- **Akira Toriyama (readable economy)** — clean shapes that read at
  thumbnail size, efficient line count, comedy timing in the acting.
  Apply: when a design doesn't read small, simplify before adding.
- **Hirohiko Araki (posed fashion)** — statuesque standing figures,
  haute-couture silhouettes, color used decoratively rather than
  naturalistically. Apply: give key frames a poster silhouette.
- **Naoki Urasawa (panel acting)** — ordinary faces, long dialogue
  held in small repeated panels so micro-changes land, thriller
  rhythm from eyeline and pause. Apply: let acting carry talky
  scenes; no splash needed.
- **Takehiko Inoue (brush and air)** — Vagabond-era brushwork,
  white space as weather and mood, motion drawn fast and loose.
  Apply: leave breathing room; one charged brush stroke beats ten
  careful ones for speed.
- **Kentaro Miura (weight through density)** — layered
  cross-hatching that makes armor, cloth, and darkness feel heavy;
  detail concentrated where the eye rests. Apply: grade hatching
  density toward the focal point, never uniform.
- **Moto Hagio / Riyoko Ikeda (shojo flow)** — layered translucent
  panels, flowers and fabric as emotional notation, close-ups that
  dissolve borders. Apply: overlap panels for memory and feeling;
  let a gaze break the grid.
- **Yoshihiro Tatsumi / Jiro Taniguchi (gekiga quiet)** — working-
  class realism, held wide shots, stories told in the gap between
  panels. Apply: underplay; a static street can carry a page.

## Comics and bande dessinee masters

- **Jack Kirby (cosmic dynamism)** — blocky coiled-spring anatomy,
  tilted foreshortened layouts, splash pages as events, Kirby
  Krackle (clustered black dots) for raw energy. Apply: exaggerate
  toward motion, never toward realism; energy gets a texture.
- **Will Eisner (sequential grammar)** — the splash page as a
  storytelling unit, city-as-character backgrounds, expressive
  body acting, gutters as timing. Apply: design the page before
  the panels; let rain, steam, and crowds act.
- **Alex Toth (design-first)** — blacks laid in as graphic shapes,
  fewest possible lines, staging that reads in silhouette. Apply:
  squint test every panel; if it fails in black-and-white shapes,
  redraw before rendering.
- **Mike Mignola (shadow masses)** — huge flat blacks, figures
  emerging from darkness, folklore weight through simplicity.
  Apply: carve light out of black instead of shading white.
- **Herge (ligne claire)** — one even-weight line, no hatching,
  researched legible backgrounds, color flat and precise. Apply:
  when clarity matters more than mood, drop rendering entirely.
- **Moebius (line metamorphosis)** — restless line weight, vast
  desert negative space, worlds that transform panel to panel.
  Apply: let environments change state; travel is a visual effect.
- **Frank Miller (noir grid-breaking)** — hard blacks, broken grids
  for impact, terse captions against loud pictures. Apply: break
  the grid only on story beats, or it means nothing.

## Animation masters

- **Hayao Miyazaki (lived layout)** — ma (held emptiness), food /
  water / work drawn truthfully, flight weight, heroines who act
  through chores. Apply: one honest everyday action per scene.
- **Isao Takahata (sketch realism)** — unfinished line allowed,
  documentary observation, emotion without melodrama. Apply: leave
  construction visible when polish would lie.
- **Satoshi Kon (subjective cutting)** — match cuts and graphic
  matches across reality/fantasy/dream, continuity errors played
  straight, subjectivity as editing. Apply: cut on shape and motion
  across scene changes; repeat with small variations.
- **Mamoru Oshii (stillness and texture)** — held wide shots,
  bird's-eye detachment, montage over dialogue. Apply: let the
  camera wait; dread and awe need duration.
- **Makoto Shinkai (light as emotion)** — skies, lens flare, and
  backlight carrying the feeling, hyper-detailed small corners.
  Apply: motivate the mood through weather and hour, then stage
  small against it.
- **Yoshinori Kanada (expressive effects)** — strong key poses,
  irregular timing for punch over smoothness, lightning and liquid-
  fire effects, constant deformation, rough thick lines. Apply:
  pose over interpolate; effects are drawn, not simulated.
- **Yutaka Nakamura (impact engineering)** — held anticipation,
  cubic debris, smear-to-pose impacts, camera shake on the hit.
  Apply: budget frames to the 6 frames around contact.
- **Hironori Tanaka (dense acting)** — Kanada-school timing inside
  long character-acting cuts, off-model allowed for flow. Apply:
  let a cut run long when the acting earns it.
- **Glen Keane / Milt Kahl (appeal through construction)** —
  force drawn inside the figure (Keane), appeal plus solid
  anatomy and lip/chin acting (Kahl). Apply: every pose must
  balance appeal against structure; never one without the other.

## Applying a master's lens

1. Name the lens in one line ("Otomo widescreen + hand-hatch
   backgrounds; original cast").
2. Translate to 3–5 tags or notes (prompt vocabulary, palette,
   timing) — never the master's characters, titles, or plots.
3. QC against the lens: would the master cut this panel/frame?
   If yes, cut it before spending on generation.
4. One lens per job; note the lens in the reply so the thread can
   hold or change it ("keeping Otomo widescreen — name another to
   switch", same mechanism as the locked style).
