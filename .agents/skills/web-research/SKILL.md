# Web Research Method

Research answers a question with evidence, not vibes. Every claim in
the answer traces to something read, and everything merely inferred
is labeled as inference.

## Strategy

- Decompose the question: what is settled, what is volatile (prices,
  versions, policies, model catalogs), what needs a primary source.
- Search the volatile parts first with distinctive terms (exact
  product names, version numbers, site:docs).
- Prefer primary sources in this order: official docs, official
  changelogs and status pages, standards bodies, reputable news with
  named authors, community consensus (multiple independent reports).
- One source is a rumor. Corroborate every load-bearing fact twice:
  two independent sources, or one primary source plus one live check.

## Reading with web_fetch

- Fetch the page, not the snippet: snippets lie by omission.
- Read the date first. Anything about models, prices, APIs, or
  policies older than a few months is suspect until re-verified.
- Extract: the exact claim, its conditions and limits, the version
  or date it applies to, and what would falsify it.
- When a page links the real evidence (docs, paper, changelog), fetch
  that next instead of quoting the middleman.

## Source evaluation

- Official docs can be stale; check the page date and changelog.
- Vendor benchmarks flatter the vendor; look for independent
  reproductions.
- Forum answers solve one person's setup; verify the version and
  platform match before applying.
- If sources disagree, say so and name the disagreement — do not
  average them into false confidence.

## Citing

- Link the exact page read, with a descriptive label, never a bare
  URL and never a URL from memory.
- Quote numbers, names, and limits exactly; paraphrase the rest.
- State the verification date for anything volatile.
- Mark single-source claims as unconfirmed.

## Deliverable shape

- Lead with the answer, then the evidence trail, then the gaps.
- Separate verified facts from inference in the wording itself.
- End with what would change the answer (the falsifier), so the
  next session knows what to re-check.
